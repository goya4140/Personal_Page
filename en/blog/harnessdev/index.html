HarnessDev: can agents select better harness versions?

Creation, feedback-driven evolution, held-out evaluation, and transfer across runtime models.

Agents can write runnable harnesses. Can they also select versions that improve beyond their development feedback? HarnessDev evaluates the runnable artifacts and separates the model that creates a harness from the one that executes it. In only 2 of 9 evolution lineages did the creator declare the best version on the held-out set. Yuhao Wu et al., arXiv v1

1. Understanding the Topic: How Should We Measure a Better Harness?

A spreadsheet agent may declare completion after inserting formulas while leaving some cells empty. A completion check could catch that case and also impede legitimate exits. Evaluation needs to account for improvement on visible tasks, improvement on unseen tasks, and execution-token cost. Another runtime model may also have different stopping and tool-calling behavior.

2. Related Work: From Optimizing a Harness to Evaluating Its Developer

The Darwin Gödel Machine studies branching search over coding-agent versions. HarnessEvolve uses reference traces to diagnose failures and screen updates. HarnessDev measures whether a developer model can create and select reproducibly runnable artifacts, and compares those artifacts under their paired models and a common executor. Related work

3. Research Questions: Creation, Evolution, and Transfer

  1. Can models turn a weak runnable seed and a few examples into an effective harness?
  2. Given task feedback, can a creator choose a version that also improves on unseen tasks?
  3. How much of a harness's performance survives a change of executor?

4. Understanding the Method: Frozen Commits Across Two Development Stages

Creation. A creator starts from a weak runnable seed and edits an executable artifact within common entry-point and audit constraints. It can debug on visible examples. An independent environment evaluates the frozen submission. Self-Eval uses the creator as executor, while Unified-Eval uses Gemini 3.1 Pro for all submissions. Paper workflow and Section 3

Evolution. The creator starts from its Code harness and improves it with feedback from 100 SWE-Pro tasks and 89 Terminal-Bench tasks. A commit enters the official trajectory only after both evaluations finish, and the feedback score equally weights the two percentages. After development, the authors evaluate each official version on 630 separate SWE-Pro tasks, withholding those scores from the creator. Sections 3 and 4.3

5. Experimental Design and Results

Main Evaluation 1: Creation Depends on the Task and Executor

Creation covers 6 creator models, 4 domains, and 5 benchmarks with 2,207 unique downstream instances. Results typically average three independent creations per creator and benchmark. Writing and machine-learning experimentation approach or exceed the selected references, while search shows a larger gap. Some references are external system results with different executors. Rankings change under a fixed executor, so Self-Eval also reflects harness–model compatibility. Tables 3–4

Main Evaluation 2: Held-Out Gains Are Smaller Than Feedback Gains

All five self-runtime declarations improve on the held-out set, by 1.43–4.44 percentage points with a mean of 3.11. Under fixed Gemini, only Opus improves and the other three lineages regress. For example, GPT-5.5's fixed-executor feedback score moves from 56.6 to 59.1, while its held-out score drops from 42.22 to 31.90. Table 6 and Figure 8

Supporting Analysis: Trace the Score Back to the Executed Change

Feedback and held-out scores move in the same direction for 34 of 64 comparable switches (53.1%). One positive case is Opus detecting that many runs claimed success without passing the task and adding a completion check. Such changes can be inspected in the execution path. With one evolution trajectory per creator–executor setting and held-out evaluation limited to SWE-Pro, the comparisons support diagnosis more directly than statistical generalization. Section 4.3 and limitations

6. Our Reflections

Topic Reflection: What Is This Field Studying?

HarnessDev makes version selection measurable. A developer needs to establish which change actually executed, whether a feedback change exceeds noise, and which executor behavior the change relies on.

Limitation Reflection: What Remains Unresolved?

Repeated use of visible tasks can amplify lucky runs. Held-out evolution covers one code benchmark, leaving writing, analysis, and research evolution untested. Specializing a harness for one model may also be useful and needs a separate assessment from portability. Section 6

Solution Reflection: What Directions Could Address These Issues?

Freeze the incumbent and candidate, rerun them with independent seeds and two executors, and select on reserved validation data. Then evaluate on a hidden set from a different task distribution, compare with a simple search baseline under matched budgets, and retain the execution paths and resource costs associated with each change.
