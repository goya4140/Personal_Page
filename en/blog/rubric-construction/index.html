Rubric construction: from scoring rules to verifiable learning signals

A framework for requirement provenance, atomic criteria, hard gates, process feedback, and adversarial audits.

This article synthesizes primary papers and author project pages on constructing and validating scoring rules. Reported findings remain scoped to each study’s settings. Cross-work comparisons and research proposals are our synthesis, not established conclusions.

Evaluating open-ended coding agents extends beyond whether a patch passes tests. Real issues combine functionality, compatibility, performance, safety, maintainability, and process constraints. Compilation, tests, and static analysis can adjudicate some requirements; others require interpreting intent, checking evidence, and accommodating multiple correct implementations. Rubric construction therefore builds a verifier combining requirements, environmental evidence, and decision rules, beyond a longer scoring prompt. Good rubrics decompose vague success into traceable, observable, falsifiable criteria. Poor ones may scale judge preferences, superficial reference structures, or generator biases.

Educational measurement offers a warning: analytic, task-specific rubrics and rater training often improve reliability, but a rubric’s existence does not establish validity. Jonsson and Svingby, 2007 Brookhart and Chen, 2015 This distinction matters even more for LLM evaluation. FLASK, G-Eval, and Prometheus show benefits from fine-grained, conditioned judges for diagnosis or human agreement, while MT-Bench identifies position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases. FLASK G-Eval Prometheus MT-Bench We therefore examine the verification chain from construction to training, beyond individual judge accuracy.

Definition: a rubric is an executable approximation of requirements

Let task requirements be (Q) and available environmental evidence (E), including issues, repositories, tests, logs, reference patches, and tool outputs. A rubric can be written as:

R={(ci,pi,vi,wi,gi)}i=1m,\mathcal{R} = \{(c_i, p_i, v_i, w_i, g_i)\}_{i=1}^{m},R={(ci​,pi​,vi​,wi​,gi​)}i=1m​,

Here, cic_ici​ is a criterion, pip_ipi​ its requirement provenance, viv_ivi​ acceptable evidence or a verifier, wiw_iwi​ its weight, and gig_igi​ its hierarchy or gate. Two implications follow. Every criterion should trace to requirements, tests, domain standards, or risk constraints, rather than generic preferences such as clarity or elegant code detached from the task. Each criterion also needs evidence that could support or refute it: tests, behavioral properties, tool-produced artifacts, or calibrated human/LLM judgments.

Coding agents particularly need separate hard gates and soft/process criteria. Hard gates cover compilation, prohibition of grader tampering, critical safety invariants, and executable tests. Soft criteria cover localization, evidence collection, minimal edits, recovery, and efficiency. A weighted sum can improperly reward fluent but functionally wrong trajectories. Conversely, final tests alone give sparse signals for long searches. SWE-TRACE trains process reward models (PRMs) using process rubrics; a rubric-supervised critic learns from sparse real outcomes. Neither removes the need for independent final execution. SWE-TRACE A Rubric-Supervised Critic

Construction chain: requirements and environmental evidence → requirement graph → atomic criteria → executable gates and process evidence → independent validation → training or search → adversarial audit and revision.

A spectrum from fixed dimensions to task-adaptive construction

Methods can be organized by rubric origin, structure, and use rather than publication year. Fixed capability dimensions include FLASK’s instruction-conditioned skill sets, G-Eval’s evaluation forms and chain-of-thought, and Prometheus’s open evaluator conditioned on explicit rubrics/references. They demonstrate more diagnostic scoring than a single overall score. Prometheus reports Pearson correlation 0.897 across 45 customized rubrics, evidence of judge alignment under particular data/rubric conditions rather than proof of rubric correctness. FLASK G-Eval Prometheus

Question-specific rubrics include SedarEval’s scoring/deduction points for 1,000 knowledge, math, coding, and logic questions; AlignBench’s Chinese scenarios, manually checked references/evidence, and rule-calibrated multidimensional judges; and Rubric Is All You Need’s comparisons on 150 DSA and 80 OOP submissions. They bring task details, deductions, and evidence into evaluation beyond generic helpfulness. SedarEval AlignBench Rubric Is All You Need Specificity also introduces authoring, reference anchoring, and maintenance costs. Criteria extracted from a gold patch’s surface structure may reject behaviorally equivalent alternatives.

Hierarchical tasks and process trajectories extend the structure further. PaperBench decomposes 20 paper-replication tasks into 8,316 individually scorable nodes, developed with original authors, to assess partial progress. PaperBench SWE-TRACE’s Rubric-Agent provides intermediate feedback for PRM training. Another critic defines 24 observable behaviors from real human–agent traces and improves SWE-bench reranking and early stopping. SWE-TRACE A Rubric-Supervised Critic Their value is turning observable intermediate evidence into learning signals, beyond replacing tests with language judges. Local behavior scores still have no automatic causal relationship with success.

Automatic generation, selection, and reconstruction includes OpenRubrics, which extracts hard rules and implicit principles from preferred/rejected differences and filters by preference-label consistency; Auto-Rubric, which builds a compressed hierarchical core from few preference pairs through verification-driven refinement; and RRD, which recursively decomposes, filters, and controls correlation weights to address missing coverage, mixed dimensions, and redundancy. OpenRubrics Auto-Rubric RRD RubricHub combines principle-guided synthesis, multi-model aggregation, and difficulty evolution into a coarse-to-fine pipeline with 110k examples. RubricRAG retrieves similar-task rubrics for domain knowledge. RubricHub RubricRAG This shifts manual bottlenecks into generation, filtering, and versioning, while introducing generator monoculture, preference biases, and retrieval leakage.

Finally, rubrics become training signals. Rubrics as Rewards uses instance-specific rubrics for on-policy RL on HealthBench and GPQA-Diamond, reporting improvements of up to 31% and 7% over direct Likert-judge rewards. OpenRS combines pairwise adaptive meta-rubrics with verifiable pointwise rubrics for pairwise RL; AdaRubric generates agent-specific dimensions from task descriptions to produce DPO preferences. Rubrics as Rewards OpenRS AdaRubric Denser training makes rubrics active optimization targets. Static test agreement therefore cannot establish RL safety: policies may exploit criteria, judge biases, or fabricate superficial tool evidence.

A construction and validation protocol for coding agents

Use specification-first, trajectory-second construction. Build a provenance-linked requirement graph from issues, interface contracts, repository constraints, tests, and historical behavior. Separate hard gates, key functionality, boundary/regression cases, quality constraints, and optional process evidence. Keep criteria atomic so a failure maps to a clear requirement. Assign the strongest available verifier: tests or static invariants before LLM judgments; use conditioned judges only where quality/process items cannot be checked directly. Validate with reference patches, different correct patches, single-violation mutations, near misses, and adversarial traces.

Limitations: what current evidence does not establish

First, most findings are correlations or downstream gains under particular benchmarks, judges, prompts, and model versions; they do not establish sufficient validity against task truth. Second, generators, filters, and judges often share model families or preference sources, allowing shared bias to appear internally stable. Third, reference conditioning can confuse reference similarity with specification satisfaction, especially for code with multiple valid solutions. Fourth, RL/reranking gains do not automatically establish safe rewards: persistent optimization changes the trajectories a judge encounters. Fifth, medical, personal-health, and real-repository uses also raise privacy, accountability, update governance, expert disagreement, and auditability issues. ClinConsensus and RubricsTree illustrate the value of expert anchors and hierarchies, while showing that automation does not erase these costs. ClinConsensus RubricsTree

Open questions and research agenda

Rubric adequacy benchmarks. The immediate gap is counterfactual testing of rubrics themselves: construct alternative passes, single-failure near misses, and adversarial bypasses per criterion, measuring coverage and discrimination through criterion-level true/false positives. RubricBench provides an expert-atomic starting point that still needs links to executable behavior and mutation testing. RubricBench

Specification-first verification with multiple correct solutions. Derive criteria from external behavior, contracts, and invariants rather than gold-patch syntax/files. A heterogeneous ensemble of property-based tests, metamorphic relations, static analysis, and semantic judges is promising; report alternative-solution acceptance explicitly.

Rubric–policy co-evolution. Once used in RL, DPO, or search, ask whether rubrics still represent objectives against actively optimizing policies. Go beyond best-of-N to frozen anchor rubrics, independent post-training tests, red-teaming, replay relabeling, and reward-hacking success rates. RaR, OpenRS, SWE-TRACE, and AdaRubric demonstrate potential while making audits prerequisites. RaR OpenRS SWE-TRACE AdaRubric

Causal validity of process signals. A behavior common in successful traces need not cause success. Compare sibling actions from identical states, or use counterfactual replay/step ablation to measure success changes after removing a process item, moving PRMs beyond language-style critics toward causal verifiers.

Independent domain, language, and harness holdouts. Generalization cannot be measured only through English paraphrases. Retain domain, language, tool/harness, judge, and generator holdouts. Chinese logic and clinical contexts deserve independent domain treatment rather than translated English benchmarks. LLMEval-Logic ClinConsensus

Human collaboration and version governance. Prioritize human effort for high-disagreement, low-confidence, high-risk, or high-reward-gradient frontier cases instead of authoring every item from scratch. Version criterion provenance, generating models/prompts, editors, validation examples, known exploits, and withdrawal histories. These support both reproducibility and high-stakes governance.

Closing perspective

Rubric construction should focus on evaluation rules remaining valid against requirements, evidence, and optimized policies. Fixed-dimension judges provide diagnostic starting points; question-specific and hierarchical rubrics bring task structure; automatic generation and rewards scale feedback while amplifying omissions, shared bias, and hacking. For coding agents, anchor final correctness in executable hard gates, provide dense feedback through atomic process rubrics, and continuously test the gap using alternative correct solutions, mutations, adversarial traces, and human review.
