A problem in. A research cycle out.

Given a scientific problem, ScientistTwo identifies baseline limitations, proposes new ideas, runs experiments, and produces a manuscript with executable code.

Two AI reviewers, different roles

Among the 86 successfully generated papers, ScientistTwo reaches 91.9% simulated acceptance under ScholarPeer and 72.1% under Stanford Agentic Reviewer. ScholarPeer guides refinement; Stanford is held out during development. These are AI-review outcomes, not conference decisions.

Table 4 reports a median gain of 7.7 and mean gain of 25.2 (both in %) for successful ScientistTwo cases. The mean should not be read as a typical gain for every task.

Why iteration matters

Figure 9 · On ICML-derived tasks, ScientistTwo gains most during early refinement. Evolved ideas dominate later selections, using both successful and failed execution traces as feedback.

A separate human check

Nine experienced reviewers assessed 33 NeurIPS-derived papers. ScientistTwo reached overall parity with human papers: 3.0 on a relative-preference scale where 3.0 means parity. Methodological rigor remained slightly behind.

A promising loop—with limits

ScientistTwo demonstrates autonomous empirical research, but does not yet consistently deliver spotlight-level conceptual breakthroughs. On the 33 NeurIPS-derived tasks used for cost analysis, the reported mean run takes about 2.5 days and costs $3,765, including tokens and virtual machines.
